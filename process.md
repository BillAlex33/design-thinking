# Design Thinking Process — Detailed Overview

Design Thinking is iterative. You can (and should) move back and forth between stages as you learn more.

## 1. Empathize

**Goal:** Gain deep understanding of the people you are designing for.

**Activities:**
- User interviews
- Contextual observation / shadowing
- Empathy maps
- Journey mapping
- Immersion in the user's environment

**Output:** Raw insights, quotes, observations, pain points.

## 2. Define

**Goal:** Synthesize what you learned into a clear, actionable problem statement.

**Activities:**
- Affinity clustering of insights
- Creating Point-of-View (POV) statements: *[User] needs [need] because [insight]*
- "How Might We" (HMW) questions

**Output:** A focused problem framing that guides ideation.

## 3. Ideate

**Goal:** Generate a large quantity of diverse ideas.

**Activities:**
- Brainstorming (defer judgment)
- Crazy 8s
- SCAMPER
- Analogous inspiration
- Voting / prioritization techniques

**Output:** A set of promising concepts to prototype.

## 4. Prototype

**Goal:** Make ideas tangible so they can be experienced and tested.

**Activities:**
- Low-fidelity sketches and paper prototypes
- Storyboards
- Role-playing
- Digital wireframes or clickable mockups
- Physical models

**Output:** Something people can interact with.

## 5. Test

**Goal:** Learn what works, what doesn't, and why.

**Activities:**
- Usability testing with real users
- Feedback sessions
- A/B comparisons
- Observation of use

**Output:** Insights that refine the solution or send you back to earlier stages.

---

Remember: the goal is not to follow the stages rigidly, but to keep users at the center while rapidly learning and improving.
