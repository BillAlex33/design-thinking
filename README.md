# Design Thinking

A practical guide to the Design Thinking methodology — a human-centered approach to innovation and problem-solving.

## What is Design Thinking?

Design Thinking is a non-linear, iterative process that teams use to understand users, challenge assumptions, redefine problems, and create innovative solutions to prototype and test. It is especially useful for tackling ill-defined or unknown problems.

Popularized by IDEO and Stanford d.school, it emphasizes empathy, collaboration, and experimentation.

## The Five Stages

1. **Empathize** — Understand the users and their needs through observation, engagement, and immersion.
2. **Define** — Synthesize findings into a clear problem statement (Point of View).
3. **Ideate** — Generate a wide range of creative ideas and solutions.
4. **Prototype** — Build inexpensive, scaled-down versions of the product or specific features.
5. **Test** — Test prototypes with users, gather feedback, and iterate.

The process is iterative — insights from later stages often send teams back to earlier ones.

## Core Principles

- **Human-centered**: Focus on real people and their needs.
- **Collaborative**: Cross-functional teams work together.
- **Optimistic**: Believe that better solutions are possible.
- **Experimental**: Embrace prototyping and learning from failure.
- **Mindful of process**: Balance divergent and convergent thinking.

## Common Tools & Methods

| Stage       | Tools & Activities                  |
|-------------|-------------------------------------|
| Empathize   | Interviews, Journey Maps, Empathy Maps, Observation |
| Define      | Affinity Diagramming, "How Might We" questions, POV statements |
| Ideate      | Brainstorming, SCAMPER, Crazy 8s, Mind Mapping |
| Prototype   | Paper prototypes, Storyboards, Role-playing, Digital mockups |
| Test        | Usability testing, A/B tests, Feedback sessions |

## Getting Started

1. Form a diverse team.
2. Pick a real challenge or problem.
3. Spend time with users (Empathize).
4. Frame the problem clearly (Define).
5. Generate many ideas, then converge (Ideate).
6. Build something tangible quickly (Prototype).
7. Test with real users and iterate.
